BINGE · TV-detalj & the duotone / raw boundary v6c · detail page

Inside a title, the rules change.

Outside the title you are navigating — scanning the library, the calendar, the recommendations. Chrome's job there is to make every poster legible at speed and visually unified. Duotone, every time.

Inside the title you are previewing — looking at the show itself. Chrome's job there is to get out of the way of the actual material. Episode stills should look like the episode. Trailers should play in full color. Cast portraits should be photographs of actors. Raw, every time.

So the boundary is sharp: duotone for identification surfaces, raw for preview content. The detail page is the one place both rules coexist — the hero poster (top-left) is duotone because it identifies; everything else (trailer, episode stills, gallery, cast) is raw because it previews. At the bottom of the page we return to duotone for "Similar series" — because that's navigation again.

This isn't a compromise. It's the system being honest about which job each image is doing.

Use duotone for
Identification surfaces
Posters in lists, tables, library grids, search results, recommendations, similar-shows, watchlists, the calendar, friend feed, hero blocks. Anything where the user is scanning among titles to choose one.
Use raw for
Preview content
Episode stills, trailer video and its poster frame, behind-the-scenes gallery, cast headshots, character art, festival prizes. Anything that's about this one title and that the user is looking at, not navigating among.

Read the page as zones: the hero poster up top is in the title's house tone — terracotta — because it identifies. The trailer, episode stills, behind-the-scenes gallery, and cast headshots are all raw because they're previews of this one show. Then "Liknande serier" at the bottom is back in duotone because the user has shifted from previewing this to navigating to another. One page, two modes, sharp boundary.

Bonus rule: trailer video plays in full color. The duotone applies to image bitmaps only, never to motion video.
